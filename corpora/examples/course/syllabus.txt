Introduction to Data Systems (CS 214) is a fictional example course used
to demonstrate the course-qa service. It covers the design of systems
that store and process data at scale, from single-machine databases to
distributed storage.

By the end of the course, students should be able to design a normalized
relational schema, explain the tradeoffs between consistency and
availability in a distributed system, and write efficient SQL queries
against a multi-table database.

The course meets Tuesdays and Thursdays from 10:00 to 11:15 in Halden
Hall room 118. There is no separate lab section this term.

Grading is based on four problem sets (40% total), a midterm exam (25%),
and a final project (35%). There is no final exam.

Late problem sets lose ten percent of the available credit per day late,
up to three days. After three days late, a problem set receives no
credit unless an extension was arranged in advance with the instructor.

Office hours are Wednesday afternoons from 14:00 to 16:00 in the
instructor's office, Halden Hall room 302. No appointment is needed.

Academic integrity: problem sets are individual work. Discussing general
concepts with classmates is encouraged, but submitted code and written
answers must be your own.
